Below is a detailed English description of the Tabernacle and its furnishings, based mainly on **Exodus 25–30**. Measurements are given in biblical **cubits** (1 cubit ≈ 18 inches / 45 cm).

---

## The Tabernacle (Exodus 25–27; 30; 35–40)

The Tabernacle was a portable sanctuary—a “Tent of Meeting”—where God dwelt among the Israelites during their wilderness journey. It was built according to the pattern shown to Moses on Mount Sinai. Its materials included gold, silver, bronze, blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, fine twisted linen, goats’ hair, ram skins dyed red, durable leather, acacia wood, oil, spices, and precious stones.

The Tabernacle was divided into:
- **The Outer Court** – the surrounding courtyard.
- **The Holy Place** – the first inner room.
- **The Most Holy Place (Holy of Holies)** – the inner sanctuary where the Ark was kept.

---

## 1. The Ark of the Covenant (Exodus 25:10–16)

- Made of **acacia wood**.
- Dimensions: **2.5 cubits long, 1.5 cubits wide, 1.5 cubits high**.
- Overlaid with **pure gold inside and out**.
- A **gold molding** around the top.
- Four **gold rings** cast on its four feet—two on each side.
- **Poles of acacia wood** overlaid with gold, inserted into the rings to carry the Ark. The poles were never to be removed.
- The Ark contained the **tablets of the Testimony** (the Ten Commandments). Later tradition also mentions Aaron’s rod and a jar of manna (Hebrews 9:4).

---

## 2. The Mercy Seat (Exodus 25:17–22)

- Made of **pure gold**.
- Dimensions: **2.5 cubits long and 1.5 cubits wide**—matching the Ark.
- Two **cherubim of hammered gold** at the two ends of the Mercy Seat.
- The cherubim faced each other, with their wings spread upward, overshadowing the Mercy Seat.
- God said: “There I will meet with you, and from above the Mercy Seat, from between the two cherubim, I will speak with you.”

---

## 3. The Table of Showbread (Exodus 25:23–30)

- Made of **acacia wood**.
- Dimensions: **2 cubits long, 1 cubit wide, 1.5 cubits high**.
- Overlaid with **pure gold**.
- A **gold molding** around it, and a **rim of a handbreadth** with a gold molding.
- Four **gold rings** at the four corners, with **acacia poles overlaid with gold** for carrying.
- Its utensils: **dishes, pans, pitchers, and bowls** for pouring drink offerings—all of pure gold.
- **Twelve loaves of showbread** were placed on the table in two rows of six, with **pure frankincense** on each row. The bread was replaced every Sabbath and eaten by the priests in the Holy Place.

---

## 4. The Golden Lampstand (Exodus 25:31–40)

- Made of **pure gold**, hammered work.
- A central shaft with **six branches** extending from its sides—three on one side and three on the other.
- Each branch had **cups shaped like almond blossoms**, with buds and flowers.
- The central shaft also had four almond-blossom cups.
- **Seven lamps** were placed on top to give light.
- Its accessories—**tongs and trays**—were of pure gold.
- It was made from **one talent of pure gold**.
- The lampstand stood in the Holy Place, opposite the Table of Showbread.

---

## 5. The Tabernacle Structure (Exodus 26)

### Curtains
- **Ten curtains** of fine twisted linen, with **blue, purple, and scarlet yarn**, woven with **cherubim**.
- Each curtain: **28 cubits long and 4 cubits wide**.
- Five curtains joined together, and the other five joined together.
- **Fifty loops of blue** on the edge of each set, and **fifty gold clasps** to join them into one covering.

### Goats’ Hair Curtains
- **Eleven curtains** of goats’ hair for the tent over the Tabernacle.
- Each: **30 cubits long and 4 cubits wide**.
- Five curtains joined, and six curtains joined, with the sixth doubled at the front.
- **Fifty bronze clasps** to join them.

### Coverings
- A covering of **ram skins dyed red**.
- Above that, a covering of **durable leather** (possibly badger or sea cow skins).

### Wooden Boards and Frame
- **Boards of acacia wood**, each **10 cubits long and 1.5 cubits wide**.
- **Twenty boards** on the south side and **twenty on the north side**.
- **Six boards** for the west side, plus **two corner boards**.
- Each board had **two silver bases**.
- Boards overlaid with gold, with **gold rings** and **bars of acacia wood overlaid with gold** to hold the frame together.
- Five bars for each side, with the middle bar running from end to end.

### The Veil
- A veil of **blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and fine twisted linen**, woven with **cherubim**.
- Hung on **four pillars of acacia wood overlaid with gold**, with **gold hooks** and **four silver bases**.
- This veil separated the **Holy Place** from the **Most Holy Place**.

### The Screen for the Entrance
- A screen for the door of the Tent, embroidered with **blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and fine twisted linen**.
- Hung on **five pillars of acacia wood overlaid with gold**, with **gold hooks** and **five bronze bases**.

---

## 6. The Bronze Altar of Burnt Offering (Exodus 27:1–8)

- Made of **acacia wood**.
- Dimensions: **5 cubits long, 5 cubits wide, 3 cubits high**—a square altar.
- Overlaid with **bronze**.
- **Horns** on its four corners, made of one piece with the altar.
- Its utensils: **pots, shovels, basins, forks, and firepans**—all of bronze.
- A **bronze grating** placed under the ledge of the altar.
- Four **bronze rings** for the **acacia poles overlaid with bronze**.
- The altar was **hollow**, made with boards.
- It stood in the Outer Court, at the entrance of the Tabernacle.

---

## 7. The Outer Court (Exodus 27:9–19)

- The courtyard was **100 cubits long and 50 cubits wide**.
- Hangings of **fine twisted linen**, **5 cubits high**.
- **Twenty pillars** and **twenty bronze bases** on the south side.
- **Twenty pillars** and **twenty bronze bases** on the north side.
- **Ten pillars** and **ten bases** on the west side.
- On the east side: **15 cubits of hangings with three pillars**, then the **gate of 20 cubits**, then another **15 cubits with three pillars**.
- The gate screen was made of **blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and fine twisted linen**, embroidered, hung on **four pillars** with **four bronze bases**.
- All pillars had **silver hooks and silver bands**, with **bronze bases**.
- All the pegs of the court were of **bronze**.

---

## 8. The Priestly Garments (Exodus 28)

### For Aaron the High Priest
- **Ephod**: made of gold, blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, and fine twisted linen. It had two shoulder pieces with **two onyx stones**, engraved with the names of the sons of Israel—six names on each stone.
- **Breastpiece of Judgment**: a square pouch, a span long and a span wide, made of the same materials. It held **twelve precious stones** in four rows:
  - Row 1: sardius, topaz, carbuncle
  - Row 2: emerald, sapphire, diamond
  - Row 3: jacinth, agate, amethyst
  - Row 4: beryl, onyx, jasper
- Each stone was engraved with the name of one of the twelve tribes.
- The breastpiece was attached with **gold chains and blue cords**.
- It contained the **Urim and Thummim**.
- **Robe of the Ephod**: all of blue, with a hole for the head. On its hem were **pomegranates** of blue, purple, and scarlet, with **gold bells** between them.
- **Tunic**: of fine linen.
- **Turban**: of fine linen.
- **Sash**: embroidered with needlework.
- **Gold Plate**: worn on the turban, engraved “**Holy to the LORD**.”

### For Aaron’s Sons
- **Tunics**, **sashes**, and **caps** for glory and beauty.
- **Linen undergarments** to cover their nakedness.

---

## 9. The Consecration of the Priests (Exodus 29)

- Aaron and his sons were brought to the entrance of the Tent of Meeting and **washed with water**.
- They were **clothed** in the holy garments.
- Aaron was **anointed with the holy anointing oil**.
- Sacrifices were offered:
  - A **bull** as a sin offering.
  - A **ram** as a burnt offering.
  - A **second ram** as the ram of ordination.
- Blood was placed on the **horns of the altar**, and on the **right ear, right thumb, and right toe** of Aaron and his sons.
- A **wave offering** was made of the breast and thigh of the ordination ram.
- The flesh was boiled and eaten with unleavened bread at the entrance of the Tent.
- The consecration lasted **seven days**.
- A **continual burnt offering** was to be offered morning and evening.

---

## 10. The Altar of Incense (Exodus 30:1–10)

- Made of **acacia wood**.
- Dimensions: **1 cubit long, 1 cubit wide, 2 cubits high**.
- Overlaid with **pure gold**.
- **Horns** on its four corners, made of one piece with it.
- A **gold molding** around it.
- Two **gold rings** under the molding, with **acacia poles overlaid with gold**.
- Placed in the Holy Place, **before the veil**, before the Mercy Seat.
- Aaron was to burn **sweet incense** on it every morning and evening.
- No strange incense, no burnt offering, no grain offering, and no drink offering were to be offered on it.
- Once a year, atonement was made on its horns with the blood of the sin offering.

---

## 11. The Bronze Basin (Exodus 30:17–21)

- Made of **bronze**.
- Its base was also of **bronze**.
- It was made from the **bronze mirrors** of the women who served at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting.
- Placed between the Tent of Meeting and the Bronze Altar.
- Aaron and his sons were to **wash their hands and feet** from it before entering the Tent or approaching the altar, so that they would not die.

---

## 12. The Holy Anointing Oil (Exodus 30:22–33)

- Made of:
  - **500 shekels of liquid myrrh**
  - **250 shekels of sweet cinnamon**
  - **250 shekels of sweet calamus**
  - **500 shekels of cassia**
  - **1 hin of olive oil**
- It was a **holy anointing oil**.
- Used to anoint the Tent of Meeting, the Ark, the Table, the Lampstand, the Altars, the Basin, and Aaron and his sons.
- It was not to be poured on ordinary people or imitated for common use.

---

## 13. The Holy Incense (Exodus 30:34–38)

- Made of equal parts of:
  - **Stacte**
  - **Onycha**
  - **Galbanum**
  - **Pure frankincense**
- Seasoned with **salt**, pure and holy.
- Some was beaten very fine and placed before the Testimony in the Tent of Meeting.
- It was most holy to the LORD.
- The people were forbidden to make any like it for personal use.

---

## Summary of Significance

The Tabernacle and its furnishings revealed God’s holiness, the seriousness of sin, and the need for mediation. Every element—the Ark, the Mercy Seat, the altars, the lampstand, the priests, and the sacrifices—pointed to the way God dwelt among His people and how they could approach Him. In Christian theology, these things are often understood as shadows pointing to Jesus Christ, who is the true Tabernacle, High Priest, and perfect sacrifice.
